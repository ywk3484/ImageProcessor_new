1. Why the current fit cannot reach a minute

The outline fit measures two things that need each other:

  • every hole (position, brightness, level, size) is fitted with the page’s hole shape (the sharp outline blurred by the page’s blur);
  • the page’s hole shape is fitted to the average of all holes, after the neighbours’ light has been taken out of every hole’s window, and taking it out needs the holes and the shape.

So the fit alternates between the two until neither moves: 10 to 14 rounds on the test pages, 43 sweeps over all 39,000 holes (a sweep fits every hole of the page once) and about 4,000 renderings of the hole shape per page.

The earlier proposal (docs/outline_fit_speed.md) made every sweep and every rendering cheaper and kept the rounds: 104 s → 10 s per page measured, 0.2 to 0.3 s estimated with a lot more work. One minute per image is 0.04 s per page for the whole machine, 0.16 s per GPU with four. Cheaper rounds do not get there. The rounds have to go.

2. The idea: leave the neighbours in

The rounds exist for one reason: the neighbours’ light is removed from the average hole, and removing it needs the hole shape that the fit is looking for. The proposal does not remove it.

Fig. 2(b) is fitted as it is, with this model: the hole shape at the centre, plus the same hole shape at every neighbour’s place. Where the neighbours stand relative to a hole is known from the hole positions alone. So the only unknown is still the one hole shape, and one least-squares fit finds it (c). No hole has to be fitted first, and nothing has to be repeated.

A small case shows what is at stake. Take three holes in a row, 8.5 px apart. The middle hole’s window reaches 7 px each way, so its edge is 1.5 px from the next hole’s centre: in (b) the average at that edge is still about 100 grey levels, against 190 at the centre and 40 between the holes. A fit that ignored the neighbours would read that light as a wide skirt of the hole itself. The existing fit subtracts it (and needs the shape to do so); the one-pass fit puts it into the model.

One detail makes this exact for any arrangement. The average is built on a grid of quarter pixels. Holes are sorted by where their centre sits inside a pixel (16 classes), and each class gets its own map of where the neighbours stand. On the test file this matters: its grid is exactly 8.5 px with a random shift per hole, so a hole’s position inside its pixel tells where its neighbours are. With one common map the fit left stripes of 2 grey levels at the neighbours (six times the noise); with the 16 maps it leaves noise (Fig. 2d). No lattice is assumed anywhere: the maps are counted from the positions found on the page.

3. The procedure, step by step

StepWhat it doesWhy it is there (measured)
1 Find holesLocal maxima of the lightly smoothed page above a level between its dark and bright ends; a parabola through three samples gives a sub-pixel start.Found every hole on 10 of 13 generated patterns and missed 0.01 to 1.2 % on the other three (300 nm squares 1.2 %, 50 nm dense holes 0.3 %), in 2 to 6 ms. The finder used so far (detect_centers on the CPU) takes 2.6 s per page.
2 Average holeEvery window is added to a quarter-pixel grid at its hole’s sub-pixel position: the plain mean, no weights, nothing estimated per hole. The neighbour maps are counted from the positions.Dividing each window by its hole’s own fitted brightness first (a bigger hole gets a bigger divisor) tilts the average towards small holes: −0.09 nm on the page mean in the test.
3 Page fitOutline and blur by least squares on the average, neighbours in the model. The existing fit’s grid of starts and stages; the gradient is computed exactly instead of by trial renderings.About 20 to 35 model evaluations per fit, where the existing fit renders the hole shape about 4,000 times per page. The same fit done the existing way (trial renderings on the CPU) took 113 s and 1,289 renderings, and landed 0.001 nm away.
4 Centre holesTwo sweeps of the existing hole stage with the size held: position, brightness and level of every hole against the complete page (all neighbours modelled at their own places).The starts from step 1 are pulled towards near neighbours. Stacking with them reads the page +0.12 to +0.17 nm too big on the copy of the test file; after this step, +0.02 nm. One sweep leaves the page CD 0.02 nm short of where it settles, two sweeps 0.004 nm.
5 Average + fit againSteps 2 and 3 with the centred holes, starting from step 3’s answer, every parameter free.This is the page’s result. A further round of 4 and 5 moves the page CD by +0.003 nm, a third by +0.001 nm (two pages); one round is proposed.
6 Size per holeThree sweeps of the existing hole stage with the size free (edge offset per hole), each step taken 1.3 times as far.A 1 nm bigger hole reads 1.002 nm bigger after these three sweeps, 0.969 after two plain ones, 1.000 after eight plain ones.

Everything is computed on the GPU in a few fused routines (one for averaging, one for the neighbour maps, one for the outline’s integrals, two for the sweeps over the holes), in single precision where the result does not depend on it. The small matrix work of the page fit runs on the CPU.

5. Accuracy, measured

5.1 Pages with known sizes

The repo’s generator draws pages whose every hole size is known (21 nm pixels, blur 65 nm, hole sizes spread by 2.5 nm, photon noise, 5,000 to 50,000 holes per page). Both fits ran on the same {{n_compared}} pages. Fig. 4 shows how far each one’s page mean is from the true mean.

  • Page mean. One pass is closer to the truth on {{n_closer}} of the {{n_compared}} pages; on the other {{n_other}} the two are equal within {{worst_other}} nm. The largest gains are on shapes the alternation does not settle on in 14 rounds: 160 × 80 nm ellipses ({{ell_op}} against {{ell_ex}} nm) and the staggered array ({{stag_op}} against {{stag_ex}} nm).
  • Per hole (table under Fig. 4), with the lens form. A 1 nm bigger hole reads 0.99 to 1.03 nm bigger (existing: 0.98 to 1.05). The scatter per hole is the same within 3 %. A 10 % brighter hole reads at most 0.08 nm bigger (existing: up to 0.23 nm).
  • Repeats. The same page fitted twice gives the same page CD to 0.0002 nm and the same hole CDs to 0.0006 nm rms (on the small dense patterns of section 5.4, where the answer is poorly determined: 0.02 to 0.07 nm). The existing fit on the GPU differs between runs by up to 0.05 to 0.1 nm on some pages.

  • One pass reads {{diff_mean}} ± {{diff_sd}} nm above the existing fit (largest difference {{diff_max}} nm). The file’s true sizes are not known. 5.5 Two possible answers (found after the proposal)

  • What happens. The fit adjusts the hole’s size and the blur’s shape together. A slightly different blur shape with a slightly different hole fits a page’s average hole almost as well, so on some pages there are two answers. Started from 81 different points on each page, the fit ends as the table shows.
  • It belongs to the outline model (one sharp outline, one free blur shape), so the existing outline_fit has the same valleys: same model, same start. Whether it ends in the same one on a given page was not tested.
  • The lens numbers of section 5.1 are flattered. The lens form starts with its lens value at 0.5 cycles per pixel, as the existing fit does; the generator drew the pages with 0.48. So on generated pages the fit starts next to the right valley. On your images nobody knows the lens’s value, and the other valley is {{lens_apart}} nm away on 100 nm holes and {{lens_apart80}} nm on 80 nm holes.
  • Which valley fits better depends on how far the model follows the neighbours’ light. With the prototype’s reach of about 21 px, the valley with the generator’s lens value fits worse than the other one on all 4 generated pages tried. With 30 px it fits better on 3 of the 4; with 40 px the fitted blur on one of them is the generator’s within 0.4 %. The longer reach costs {{reach_ms}} ms per page and does not move the answer inside a valley (0.02 nm at most).
  • A further, false gauss2 answer came from a defect of my prototype, now fixed. The form’s two Gaussians can swap roles, and a fit that drifted into the swapped arrangement stopped at a limit, short of the best fit. On small images (135 to 5,000 holes) the page CD then depended on which hole positions the fit was handed, by 0.4 to 0.6 nm; with the fix by 0.005 nm. On full pages it happened on 2 of 58 and moved one of them by 0.02 nm; no number on this page changed (largest change on the test file: 0.0004 nm).
  • For your test on real images. A difference between outline_fit_fast and outline_fit of about 0.2 nm on 100 nm holes (0.6 nm on 80 nm holes) can come from this alone, with nothing wrong in either procedure. Each image’s report line will show the blur’s second part for both methods, so that it can be seen. Compare with gauss2 first: after the fix it has one answer on {{gauss_single}} of the 10 pages above, the lens form on {{lens_single}}.

6. What changes in your numbers

  • Page means move by a few hundredths to a few tenths of a nanometre against the existing outline fit (section 5), towards the truth on the generated pages. Results of the two must not be mixed in one comparison.
  • The page CD is the size of the average hole. When hole sizes spread widely, that is above the average of the sizes by (spread)² / (2 × CD): 0.03 nm at a 2.5 nm spread, 0.125 nm at 5 nm (measured: {{cd5}}). The existing fit has the same property.
  • The blur’s second part is held near its default where a page does not determine it (gauss2 form: a second, wider Gaussian that the page’s blur may not have). Its width and weight are then arbitrary. On the test file, where the fit gives the second part under 7 % of the light, the CD does not depend on them (±0.002 nm over the whole range, measured); on pages with another blur shape it can (section 5.5). The existing fit lets them wander, which is why its blur readings jump from page to page.
  • Holes are found by the fit’s own finder, not by the report’s segmentation. In the library, the holes that the other methods measure would be matched to it by position.
  • Nothing else is new: same outline (superellipse with harmonics), same blur forms, same hole stage, same CD definition.

7. What these tests do not show

No real ACTIS page was used, because none is on this PC. The test TIFF (artifacts/cd_test/data/stripe1_board1.tif) is generated as well: its holes sit on a grid of exactly 8.500 px with a random shift of up to ±0.3 px each, and the repo’s own fixture script calls its pages synthetic. Earlier notes of mine called it real. It is not.

  • Real pages. A real blur’s far tail, a real background, defects, and real hole shapes are untested. Generated pages obey the fit’s model by construction, so they can show that the procedure is right, not that the model fits your images. That holds for the existing outline fit just as much.
  • The remote. Nothing was timed there. Four GPUs at once were not tried (this PC has one).
  • Pages without the pattern. The fit does not recognise them: on the test file’s blank pages it finds 27,000 noise peaks and fits them. The hole check has to stay in front of it. On the file’s page with another pattern the page fit’s leftover is 15 times the noise, which is a usable alarm; on the blank pages nothing looks wrong except a fitted blur of 14 nm.
  • Small holes. Section 5.4.
  • Which of two answers is the right one on a real page (section 5.5). On generated pages the truth is known; on yours it is not, and the model cannot tell by itself.
  • Not in the prototype yet: measuring only a part of a page (your tiles), dark holes on a bright page, the switch that gives every hole the page’s size when holes are too small for a size each, the x and y widths per hole, the workbench’s outline drawing.

8. In the batch

  • Flow. One reader reads the image front to back, as today. Your hole check decides per page which area holds the pattern. The fit takes the page and that area: holes are found on the whole page (so that light from outside the area is modelled), and only holes inside the area, one window away from its border, are averaged and reported.
  • Part of a page. Tested with the pattern ending inside the page (holes on the left 40 %): the page mean is {{part_op}} nm from the truth (existing: {{part_ex}} nm).
  • One process per GPU, 20 pages per call. No worker pool per GPU (Windows gives several processes turns on one GPU; measured earlier).
  • Per page it would write: holes found and used, mean CD, x and y widths, blur, the page fit’s leftover against the noise, the change between the first and the final page fit, and with step 6 the holes measured and the share at the size bound. The per-hole values can be kept or dropped.

10. The build

The plan a fresh session builds from is docs/outline_fit_fast_handoff.md. In short:

  1. First: library and folder reports. A new method outline_fit_fast beside outline_fit (nothing replaced), on the GPU, giving the image’s mean CD, x and y widths and blur. Both folder reports get a flag for it. With both methods switched on, a run ends with one line that compares them over the folder.
  2. Then your check on real images: one command on the remote, one line to type back.
  3. After your verdict: the TIFF batch. The area of each page from the hole check (your tiles as they are, or a hole-by-hole check against the reference’s average hole that the fit can do itself: to be tried beside the tiles first), one process per GPU with 20 pages per call.
  4. Only if the method proves itself: a size per hole (step 6).

Three things turned up while the handoff was prepared. Two were defects and are fixed in the prototype: on images whose hole pitch is a whole number of pixels the first fit could land on a nonsense answer, and the gauss2 form could stop in a swapped arrangement of its two Gaussians (section 5.5). The third is not a defect and stays: on some pages the fit has two possible answers (section 5.5). An earlier version of this page said that the lens form does not depend on its start; that was wrong.
